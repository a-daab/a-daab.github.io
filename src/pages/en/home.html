Woven on looms that use no electricity.

The face of every YogaMaty is hemp thread and strips cut from reclaimed post-consumer saris, woven together on a hundred-year-old loom. Its backing is a separate handwoven hemp cloth, woven on a second hundred-year-old loom, then washed and sun-dried. The bound edge and knotted fringe are sewn on after each mat is checked by hand.

Both looms run on no electricity at all. Some other stages do use power — the finishing is done on an industrial sewing machine — which is why the passport states electricity stage by stage, never as a blanket claim for the whole product.

50 million people are trapped in modern slavery.

The most accurate statistics to date indicate that 50 million people are trapped in modern slavery worldwide — and the number grew by 10 million between 2016 and 2021.1 Many of them are trapped inside supply chains, producing clothing, electronics and food for global markets.

Modern slavery and the climate crisis are tangled in a vicious cycle: forced labor is most heavily concentrated in environmentally destructive industries, and the disasters those industries fuel leave people in the kind of desperation traffickers exploit.2
