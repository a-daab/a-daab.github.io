Handwoven from natural hemp and reclaimed sari textiles.

YOGAMATY is a premium, traditionally handwoven yoga mat towel/cover produced using natural hemp yarn and reclaimed sari textiles. The product brings together traditional weaving knowledge, agricultural materials, textile recovery, and contemporary consumer demand.

YOGAMATY is a yoga mat towel/cover that is placed directly on top of an existing conventional yoga mat. It creates a washable textile layer between the practitioner’s body and the conventional mat underneath. The conventional mat continues to provide cushioning and support. YOGAMATY provides the textile surface. It is not intended to be used as a standalone yoga mat.

Conscious Consumer-Funded Production.

The foundation of YOGAMATY is what we are calling Conscious Consumer-Funded Production. Instead of producing large quantities of products in advance and then attempting to sell them, the consumer begins the process by pre-purchasing a YOGAMATY. That purchase helps provide the working capital needed to initiate production. The intention is to connect one consumer with one individual artisan production opportunity.

The consumer is not making a donation. The consumer is not providing a loan. The consumer is purchasing a product. But the way that purchase is organized is intended to create a more direct, transparent, and equitable relationship between the consumer and the people whose labor produces it. Target delivery within 60 days of the original pre-purchase.

Why YOGAMATY?

Yoga has become a global practice associated with mindfulness, well-being, physical discipline, and spiritual awareness. Yet many of the products used in modern yoga practice are manufactured through complex global supply chains that consumers know very little about.

A person may be deeply conscious of what they eat, how they live, and the values they bring to their yoga practice, while knowing almost nothing about the people who made the products they use. YOGAMATY is intended to bring greater consciousness to that relationship.

The product is designed for serious yoga practitioners who care not only about the quality of their practice but also about the integrity of the products they bring into that practice.

The consciousness we bring to our practice should also extend to the people and materials behind the products we use.

Not simply conscious consumption. Conscious participation.

1

Where the money goes

Conscious consumption determines where the money goes. The consumer’s purchase is intended to help initiate a production opportunity.

2

How the money is used

Conscious capitalism determines how the money is used. The product is made through traditional craftsmanship.

3

Making it visible

TransparaTrade seeks to make the value of each contribution and the fairness of the exchange visible. The supply-chain system seeks to document the events and economic exchanges involved.

The larger purpose is to demonstrate that purchasing can become a more conscious form of participation in the relationships behind global trade.
