{# ───────── 2. The product ───────── #}
{{ t('home.product_eyebrow') }}

The conscious layer between you and your yoga mat.

YOGAMATY™ is a premium yoga mat towel/cover, handwoven from natural hemp and reclaimed sari textiles on traditional, electricity-free handlooms by independent women artisans in Nepal, in partnership with the survivor-led anti-slavery organization Empowerment Collective. It is the first pilot of TransparaTrade™ — an initiative to document every step of a product’s supply chain and make it visible to the person who buys it.

It is placed directly on top of your existing conventional yoga mat, creating a washable textile layer between your body and the mat underneath. Your conventional mat keeps providing the cushioning and support; YOGAMATY provides the textile surface. It is not a standalone yoga mat.

Yoga has become a global practice of mindfulness, well-being and discipline, yet many of the products used in it come from supply chains consumers know very little about. The consciousness we bring to our practice should also extend to the people and materials behind the products we use.

Conscious Consumer-Funded Production.

Instead of producing large quantities in advance and then trying to sell them, the process begins with you: you pre-purchase a YOGAMATY, and that purchase helps provide the working capital to start production. One consumer, one individual artisan production opportunity.

You are not making a donation and not providing a loan. You are purchasing a product — but the way the purchase is organized creates a more direct, transparent and equitable relationship between you and the people whose labor produces it. Made to order; target delivery within 60 days.

{{ t('home.why_eyebrow') }}

50 million people are trapped in modern slavery.

While the veiled nature of modern slavery makes it impossible to know the full extent of global exploitation, the most accurate statistics to date indicate that 50 million people are trapped in modern slavery worldwide — and the number grew by 10 million between 2016 and 2021.1 Many of them are trapped inside supply chains, producing clothing, electronics and food for global markets.

Modern slavery and the climate crisis are tangled in a vicious cycle: forced labor is most heavily concentrated in environmentally destructive industries, and the disasters those industries fuel leave people in the kind of desperation traffickers exploit.2
