The larger movement

Make supply-chain transparency a basic expectation of global trade.

The larger movement behind this project is End Modern Slavery Now! The movement seeks to challenge the systems that allow human exploitation to remain hidden within global commerce. A central objective is to advocate mandatory supply-chain transparency labeling.

Consumers are often provided information about what a product contains and how to care for it. Yet they may know very little about the people whose labor created the product. The movement seeks to make supply-chain transparency a basic expectation of global trade.

The objective is not simply to encourage companies to make voluntary ethical claims. It is to advocate systems in which meaningful supply-chain information must be documented, communicated, and subject to accountability. Mandatory labeling would require appropriate public policy, verification standards, enforcement, and worker protections.

The argument — modern slavery

Modern slavery is hidden in supply chains.

While the veiled nature of modern day slavery makes it impossible to know the full extent of global exploitation, the most accurate statistics to date indicate that 50 million people are trapped in modern slavery worldwide — and the number grew by 10 million between 2016 and 2021.1

Many of these people are trapped inside supply chains, producing clothing, electronics and food for global markets.

Survivors face a second barrier.

Survivors face poverty, interrupted education, social stigma, and sometimes even criminalization, which make it difficult to secure an empowering job and break the cycle of exploitation.

The climate connection.

Modern slavery and the climate crisis are tangled in a vicious cycle. Changes in climate and resulting disasters — droughts, flooding, wildfires, and more — upend people’s livelihoods, leaving them in extreme poverty and vulnerability.

Traffickers take advantage of this desperation for work. A farmer could fall into debt — and then debt bondage — because of drought-induced crop failure. Or a rugmaker could lose their entire stock of rugs, their equipment, and their home in a flash flood, forcing them to migrate and accept a job offer from an unknown recruiter falsely promising a living income and good working conditions.2

Forced labor is most heavily concentrated in environmentally destructive industries: mining, deforestation, fast fashion, unsustainable farming, and many others. The resource depletion and greenhouse gas emissions within these industries’ supply chains feed climate change and fuel an increase in disasters. The cycle continues.

Conscious consumption.

With each dollar we spend, we vote for the world we want to live in and we shape the future for generations to come. Conscious consumption is the practice of making informed, ethical purchasing decisions that support companies prioritizing human rights and environmental sustainability. With transparency labeling, our choices become informed decisions rather than hopeful guesses, and through TransparaTrade, individual purchases become an organized call for legal reform.

Consumer choice matters. If it didn’t, companies wouldn’t spend money on advertising.

Conscious consumption and conscious capitalism

YOGAMATY™ seeks to connect conscious consumption with a more transparent understanding of how purchasing power is used. Conscious consumption determines where the money goes. Conscious capitalism determines how the money is used. TransparaTrade seeks to make the value of each contribution and the fairness of the exchange visible. The consumer’s purchase is intended to help initiate a production opportunity. The product is made through traditional craftsmanship. The supply-chain system seeks to document the events and economic exchanges involved. The larger purpose is to demonstrate that purchasing can become a more conscious form of participation in the relationships behind global trade.
