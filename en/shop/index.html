MadeBy women artisans in the Kathmandu Valley, Nepal, on hundred-year-old looms that use no electricity

The face of the mat is hemp thread and strips cut from reclaimed post-consumer saris, woven together on a hundred-year-old loom. It is backed with a separate handwoven hemp cloth — a genuine feature of the mat, and one that keeps it from being just a surface. The bound edge and knotted fringe are sewn on after every piece is checked by hand.

Because the weft comes from reclaimed saris, no two mats are alike: the stripes, the colors and the rhythm of the weave differ from piece to piece. That is the imperfection that proves it was made by hand.

Open the supply chain passport to see every stage of how a YogaMaty is made — or scan the label on the mat when it arrives.

Three reasons

Ethics and transparency. The supply chain is fully visible to consumers, revealing that human dignity, sustainability, and living wages are prioritized at every stage.

Energy. The energy that flows through your yoga practice should not be grounded on a mat that holds the energy of suffering, extraction, and toxic materials. YogaMaty allows yogis to weave their practice with the energy of empowerment and environmental protection.

Health. YogaMaty is woven with natural hemp fibers and reclaimed sari fabric for safe and healthy interaction with skin.
