The supply chain

Two material journeys, every production stage, and the documentation we are building to make each one visible.

Two material journeys

One material comes from agricultural production. The other comes from the recovery of existing textiles.

YOGAMATY™ is particularly suitable as a TransparaTrade pilot because it combines two distinct material journeys. The objective is to demonstrate how different types of supply chains can be documented through a common technical language.

Weaving at the loom — independent artisans

Stage with no powered equipmentStage that may use electricity

The hemp supply chain begins with agricultural production. The intention is to document the agricultural source and the successive stages through which the material passes. This includes the people involved, the materials transferred, the work performed, and the economic exchanges.

The reclaimed sari supply chain begins at the earliest documented point of recovery. The project seeks to recognize the work involved in recovering and preparing materials that might otherwise be discarded. It also seeks to create fair-exchange opportunities for people participating in material recovery. The original manufacturing history of a reclaimed sari may be unknown.

Honesty about today’s records

Hand-entered, and we say so.

Current records are entered by hand. That is exactly the weakness TransparaTrade exists to fix: when companies enter their own data by hand, those not committed to ethics can enter false data.

The stages below are the planned documentation framework, shown with placeholder media. They are marked planned until records are published, batch by batch, in the Supply-Chain Explorer.

YOGAMATY is never shown with a verified Green label until the criteria and verification processes exist. See the proposed labeling concept.

Traditional production and electricity

What uses power, and what doesn’t.

Traditional handloom weaving is central to the YOGAMATY production process. The project seeks to preserve traditional production methods and reduce unnecessary reliance on industrial machinery. However, the complete production process should not be described as electricity-free. Some stages, including sewing and certain bobbin-winding activities, may use electricity. The intention is to document these activities accurately and potentially use solar panel technology to exclude electricity.

The project plans to measure electricity consumption per production batch where feasible — weaving stages are genuine zeroes. It also seeks to document labor hours, material quantities, packaging weight, transportation, and other relevant production data. These details are being further developed through amendments to the TransparaTrade whitepaper. The objective is not simply to make environmental claims. It is to create evidence that supports those claims.

Data comes in three classes: per batch (harvest, hours per stage, metered electricity, batch media, shipping records — per-piece averages are labeled as averages); per piece (serial, assembly date, any piece-specific media); and standard (construction, loom type, dimensions, care, packaging specification — with the date last checked).

Privacy, identity and worker protection

Document the steps. Protect the person.

The intention of TransparaTrade is to prove that a documented step occurred while protecting the people involved. The system should not become a tool for unnecessary surveillance. It should protect workers’ identities, private information, and physical safety.

Artisans remain anonymous; if anyone appears in published media, official documented consent is obtained first. Because weaving happens at home, no record or image may reveal where an artisan lives.

The project has considered privacy-preserving identification methods and the possible use of biometrics where appropriate. Any biometric approach would require informed consent, legal compliance, meaningful alternatives, and strong safeguards. Offline documentation, QR codes, photographs, and later synchronization may be important where reliable internet access is unavailable.
