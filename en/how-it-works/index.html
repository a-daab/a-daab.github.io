How it works

One consumer. One artisan. One pre-purchase. One YOGAMATY. One transparent supply chain.

Conscious Consumer-Funded Production

The foundation of YOGAMATY™.

The foundation of YOGAMATY is what we are calling Conscious Consumer-Funded Production. Instead of producing large quantities of products in advance and then attempting to sell them, the consumer begins the process by pre-purchasing a YOGAMATY. That purchase helps provide the working capital needed to initiate production. The intention is to connect one consumer with one individual artisan production opportunity.

The consumer is not making a donation. The consumer is not providing a loan. The consumer is purchasing a product. But the way that purchase is organized is intended to create a more direct, transparent, and equitable relationship between the consumer and the people whose labor produces it.

Target delivery within 60 days of the original pre-purchase.

The independent-artisan model

Independent women artisans and cottage industry.

The project seeks to build on traditional cottage-industry production. Rather than requiring all artisans to work in one centralized factory, the intention is to support women who can produce independently from their homes.

This approach recognizes that many women face barriers to conventional employment, including household responsibilities, transportation challenges, limited financial resources, and social restrictions. Traditional handloom production can provide an opportunity for women to participate in paid work while preserving skills that have been passed down through generations.

The proposed system includes a mobile vehicle that can deliver materials, facilitate access to looms and training where appropriate, and collect completed products. The central production team coordinates materials, quality standards, records, and fulfillment. The intention is to strengthen the independence of women artisans while connecting their work with global consumer demand.

Approximately 20 artisans have contributed to the initial development. The estimated production capacity in Kathmandu is approximately 50 products per batch, subject to further operational confirmation.

Fair exchange and living income

The Indigenous-Based Fair-Exchange and Living-Income Model.

This is one of the most important distinctions in the project. We do not want YOGAMATY to be presented simply as an economic model. The intention is to develop an Indigenous-Based Fair-Exchange and Living-Income Model. The idea is rooted in recognizing the value of traditional knowledge, individual contribution, community relationships, cultural preservation, and fair exchange.

The project seeks to recognize the contribution of everyone involved: the farmers who grow hemp, the individuals who process and spin the fibers, the people who recover and prepare discarded sari textiles, the artisans who weave the product, and those who coordinate production, transportation, technology, and distribution. The intention is that the value of each contribution should be recognized and documented.

Fair exchange should not be determined only by the institutional position, academic credentials, or financial power of a participant. It should also recognize the actual knowledge, labor, skill, and value that the participant contributes.

The project also seeks to create a pathway toward living income. Living income means more than a payment for completing a task. It must be understood in relation to the resources required for a dignified life. This is where Empowerment Collective’s Seven Keys become central.

The Seven Keys

  1. Nature
  2. Water
  3. Food
  4. Housing
  5. Medical Care
  6. Education
  7. Technology

These Seven Keys provide the intended framework for evaluating whether an individual’s economic participation supports the basic conditions necessary for a dignified and independent life. The project seeks to move beyond measuring economic opportunity solely through wages or the number of products produced. The question is whether the work contributes meaningfully toward a life in which the Seven Keys are accessible.

An objective, not an outcome. This is the living-income objective behind YOGAMATY. It is an aspiration that requires measurement, local benchmarks, and transparent evidence. It should not be represented as an outcome already achieved simply because a proposed artisan payment has been established. The amount paid for each stage will be published per hour in local currency; if a figure cannot be published, we publish who verified it and how. See The Supply Chain.

Cultural participation

Not cultural appropriation. Cultural participation.

Yoga has historical roots in the Indian subcontinent. Traditional handloom weaving also remains an important living practice in Nepal and the surrounding region. YOGAMATY seeks to connect these traditions through a contemporary product made by women artisans who contribute their own knowledge and craftsmanship.

The purpose is not to take cultural knowledge and remove the people who preserve it from the value created. The purpose is to create meaningful participation. The artisans are not simply producing something for an outside brand. Their knowledge, skills, labor, and cultural heritage are central to the product itself. This philosophy should guide how YOGAMATY is described, photographed, priced, marketed, and documented.

Pilot status

What remains to be finalized.

Several important matters remain to be finalized. These matters should be presented as development work rather than completed outcomes.

  • Product: final retail price, finished weight, durability testing, and confirmed material composition.
  • Production: verified artisan capacity, actual working hours, compensation structure, and living-income assessment.
  • Fulfillment: international shipping arrangements, customs, and confirmation of the 60-day delivery target.
  • Technology: programming-language architecture, verification methods, immutable record design, and privacy safeguards.
  • Labeling: published criteria for Green, Yellow, and Red transparency classifications.
  • Payments: final payment-processing provider and integration.
  • Whitepaper: completion and approval of the current amendments.
